I looked through your sample transcript.

Your current extraction is actually quite good for semantic search, but it's not designed for synchronization. Those are two different problems.

Right now your pipeline is essentially:

PDF
    ↓
Clean
    ↓
Group by Speaker
    ↓
Extract QA
    ↓
Store QA chunk
    ↓
Embed

That is perfect for asking questions like:

"What did the witness say about the accident?"

But synchronization has a different goal:

"When the audio reaches this exact question, highlight it."

Those require different data.

The biggest thing you're throwing away

Suppose page 25 contains

Page 25

Q. What caused you to fall?

A. I slipped on black ice.

Your extractor probably stores

{
    "question": "What caused you to fall?",
    "answer": "I slipped on black ice."
}

Looks good.

But you've lost

page number
line numbers
where it appeared
reading order
its relationship to the original page

Once that's gone, you can't accurately reconnect the audio to the document.

What I would store instead

Don't replace your current data.

Add another layer.

For every extracted block store metadata.

Example

{
  "id": 842,

  "page": 25,

  "speaker": "Q",

  "text": "What caused you to fall?",

  "bbox": [72,215,510,242],

  "paragraph_index": 94,

  "reading_order": 842
}

Answer

{
  "id":843,

  "page":25,

  "speaker":"A",

  "text":"I slipped on black ice.",

  "bbox":[72,245,510,272],

  "paragraph_index":95,

  "reading_order":843
}

Notice something important.

The Q&A extraction still exists.

But underneath it there is a document model.

Then your QA becomes

Instead of

{
   question:"...",
   answer:"..."
}

Store

{
    question_block:842,

    answer_block:843,

    objection_blocks:[844]
}

Now everything references actual document blocks.

One more suggestion

I actually would not synchronize the final Q&A objects.

I would synchronize the speaker blocks.

Why?

Imagine this exchange:

Q. ...

MR. SMITH:
Objection.

MR. JONES:
Response.

THE REPORTER:
One at a time.

A. ...

Your current Q&A model treats that as one question and one answer with an objection attached.

But in the audio, those are five separate speaking events. The timestamps belong to each speaking turn, not to the merged Q&A object.

So I'd define a SpeechBlock as the atomic synchronization unit:

SpeechBlock
------------
id
page
speaker
text
type (Q, A, Attorney, Judge, Reporter, Witness)
reading_order
bbox (for PDF)

Then:

Synchronization → SpeechBlock
RAG/Search → QAPair
Analytics → SpeechBlock
UI Highlighting → SpeechBlock

That separation gives you a much cleaner architecture and will make future features—like speaker navigation, timeline views, and precise audio jumping—much easier to implement.
